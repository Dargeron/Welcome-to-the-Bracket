She was Female Student 2, and she had sixty-eight minutes to live, most of which she enjoyed.

Episode eleven, a hallway. Female Student 1 says, “Did you hear about the transfer student?” and Female Student 2 nods. That was all there was of her. She thought she had nodded well.

Her room was a classroom, and there was a boy in it. She knew at once which of them it would be. He had the window seat, second from the back. He had two highlights in each eye. When he turned his head, his hair took a moment to follow. She had the same face as the girl who sat behind her, and her hair was one piece.

She never found out what the boy was. He had killed several thousand people and had not been in the room for one of them. He did it in writing. All he had ever needed was a face and a name, and he had her face already. He had allowed forty seconds for the rest.

She had planned to nod through the hour, since nodding was her strength, but nodding turned out not to count. Her mouth opened and said hello.

“Hello,” he said. “What’s your name?”

Nobody had asked her before. She went to get it and there was nothing in the place it should have been. She knew she had a name the way she knew she had a mother. She could feel her in the next room, but when she went to look, nothing was there.

She told him she was listed as Female Student 2. He smiled and said that was more of a job title.

He was good at it. He asked what was written inside her indoor shoes. She took one off and they both looked: a squiggle. Her notebook was full of squiggles in her own handwriting, which is what gets drawn when the words are too small to matter. He spent some time on the notebook, out of professional interest.

He asked about roll call. There is one in episode four, off screen, while the camera is on the main boy looking at a cloud. Fourth from the end there is a name that nobody on screen answers to, and she had always thought that might be her, answering out of frame. It ends in “ko.”

He said names that end in “ko,” one at a time, and watched her face. Faces were the other thing she was good at, and she gave every name a fair try. Tomoko felt close. He said it again and it didn’t.

He said he would need the family name too. She asked what for, and he smiled. She was not as slow as she was drawn. People who want to know who they’re talking to don’t keep checking a watch. He needed it for after the hour, for whatever he did to win, and she thought: fine. If he found it she would hear her whole name said out loud by a boy with highlights, and she did not expect a better offer.

With ten minutes left he ran out of names. He leaned back and looked out the window. She said she was sorry. He said it wasn’t her fault, and stopped using the nice voice.

“You aren’t a person,” he said at minute fifty-eight. His hair had fallen down in front of his face, which made him look younger and much less like a class representative. “You’re furniture. Why would they put furniture in here?”

Then the hour was up. He stood up and looked at his black notebook one more time, just in case one of the *Sachikos* had started working late, and when it hadn’t, he put the notebook carefully inside his coat pocket so the chalk dust wouldn’t mark it. Then he looked down at his own hands. They were very clean hands, with pink fingernails trimmed straight across, the kind of hands that only ever turn pages or hold chopsticks or click a ballpoint pen. He flexed his fingers twice, looking embarrassed, like a boy who had come to gym class in his dress shoes, and started walking between the desks toward her, looking slightly to the left of her ear.

He took off his tie and wound it once around each hand. He asked her to face the board, and she did, because he was the kind of boy you did that for.

He was new at it. She could tell from how he kept changing his grip. She had decided to let him, but nobody had told her hands, and they went up and got hold of his sleeve, and one of them scratched him. “Stop that,” he said, the way you’d say it to someone talking in class. She tried to. It took a long time.

She had the eyebrows up when the room went.
