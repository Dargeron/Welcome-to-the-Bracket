Fujiwara no Sai is the ghost of a Heian-era Go player in *Hikaru no Go*. In [[E0006]] he had been dead for about a thousand years and his fingers went through the stones, as they had for all that time; a boy in Edo, and later a boy called Hikaru, had placed them for him.

## Rounds 1–5

No encounter has been recorded for Sai's first five rooms.

## Round 6: the veranda

In [[E0006]] he arrived on the veranda of an old house and saw the Go board before he saw [[Meruem]]. He knelt in front of it and cried. When Meruem told him he would be killed after the hour, Sai thanked him and asked, "Do you play?"

He told Meruem that he had taught the game to an emperor, about the teacher at court who had said he cheated, about the river afterwards, and about the Hand of God, the move nobody had yet played. If he won, he said, he would ask to play Meruem's gungi opponent, and he offered to learn gungi. Meruem placed his stones where his fan pointed, and Sai won the first game.

At sixty minutes Meruem's tail passed through his chest and touched nothing. "I'm sorry," Sai said. "It's your move."

He taught Meruem for the rest of the room, as he had taught Hikaru: overjoyed when Meruem did something right, and sulking with his sleeves over his face when Meruem wanted to stop for the night. He cried again when Meruem won a game in the fourth year.

In the nineteenth year Meruem played a stone, learned from the girl, that was better than anything Sai would have played there. Sai asked to see it placed again, and again, and the third time watched the claw instead of the stone. "Hikaru did something like that once," he said, "when I wasn't looking." They did not finish the game. Around dusk he closed his fan and held it out across the board, handle first.

The encounter does not describe how he stopped. The room ended there, and Sai stopped in Round 6.
