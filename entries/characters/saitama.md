Saitama is the hero of *One-Punch Man*, who defeats any opponent with a single punch and is bored by it. He lost his hair in the training that made him strong.

## Rounds 1–12

No encounter has been recorded for Saitama's first twelve rooms.

## Round 13: the bus stop

In [[E0017]] he was sitting on the bench of a bus stop at the edge of an unfinished town, where the timetable said the last bus had left forty years ago, when [[Mumen Rider]] arrived on a bicycle. During the hour Mumen Rider asked how he had become so strong. Saitama told him: a hundred push-ups every day, then sit-ups, then running, for three years. "Also my hair fell out." He said he had been hit once, by a man who was not even trying. Asked whether he had to try, he said, "I have to try. I just win," and that it was boring: "Everything ends the first time. Nobody gets to be the next part."

When the hour was up he told Mumen Rider that he could now put him through the bus shelter, and said he didn't think he would. They stayed. He did his push-ups on the gravel each morning while Mumen Rider rode round him and counted, and they ate rice and soup at a café that had appeared at the end of the road. Mumen Rider spent a summer teaching him to ride the bicycle. He went into the ditch twice; by the second winter he rode the length of the road with no hands.

Over the years the push-ups fell from a hundred to fifty to twenty, and in about the fortieth year he moved them to the afternoons. His knees went, then his back, then his grip. Near the end he said the hair had been the first thing to go. When Mumen Rider said it wasn't, he agreed, almost smiling: "It was the push-ups."

He died on the bench in the fifty-first year, in the afternoon, with his hands on his knees. He stopped in Round 13.
